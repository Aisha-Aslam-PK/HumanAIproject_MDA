The decision of the UK to leave the EU has had profound implications for the City of London, the UK's financial heart and one of the world's foremost financial hubs. Brexit has questioned the City's position, given the loss of passporting rights which allowed seamless financial trade across the EU. Financial services firms have had to adapt to a new reality, where access to the single market is not as unfettered as before.

The passporting system enabled UK-based financial firms to sell their services across the European Union without the need for additional regulatory clearance. The loss of this system means that UK financial services firms now face a patchwork of regulations and need to navigate the differing rules of each EU member state. To mitigate this loss, many banks and financial institutions have set up subsidiaries within the EU to maintain their access to the single market.

However, the UK has been negotiating equivalence decisions with the EU, which would recognize each other’s regulatory systems as sufficiently aligned to allow for cross-border trade. Yet, this process has been slow and uncertain, with few areas covered so far, and equivalence can be unilaterally withdrawn by the EU, which does not provide the stable regulatory environment businesses crave.

The impact of these changes has also been felt in staffing and location strategies. There has been a shift of jobs and assets from London to various European cities such as Frankfurt, Paris, and Dublin. Despite this, the exodus has not been as large as initially feared, and London retains much of its workforce and allure as a global financial center.

Furthermore, the UK government sees Brexit as an opportunity to diverge from EU regulations and to make London even more attractive to international investors. It aims to achieve this through regulatory reforms intended to enhance the competitiveness of the UK financial market. This includes reviewing the listing rules to attract tech companies and startups, and considering looser capital requirements to free up banks’ balance sheets.

However, these opportunities come with risks. Divergence from EU regulations could lead to further barriers to trade with the EU and could impact London’s long-standing reputation for regulatory stability and high standards. 

Brexit has forced the City of London to confront unprecedented challenges. While it has undoubtedly lost some of its privileges in the EU financial market, the city's depth of talent, its legal system, and its global financial infrastructure continue to be strong assets. The coming years will show whether London can redefine its role and retain its status as a leading financial center in the post-Brexit era.
