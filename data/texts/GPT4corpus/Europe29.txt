The decision of the United Kingdom to leave the European Union marked a pivotal moment in the history of British immigration policy. Brexit was, to a significant extent, a referendum on freedom of movement, a fundamental principle of the EU that had allowed EU citizens to live and work in the UK with few restrictions. With the end of this arrangement, the UK had to devise a new immigration system that reflected its post-Brexit identity, balancing control over its borders with the economic need for foreign labor.

The post-Brexit points-based immigration system, introduced in early 2021, aimed to attract people who can contribute to the UK economy, regardless of their nationality. It prioritized skills and talents over nationality, a shift from the EU preference-based system. This new framework represented a reclaiming of sovereignty in the eyes of those who advocated for tighter controls on immigration, believing it would lead to lower numbers of new arrivals and more selective, merit-based criteria.

However, the new system has not been without its critics. There have been concerns about sectors traditionally reliant on EU labor, such as agriculture, healthcare, and hospitality, which are now facing labor shortages. These industries have struggled to adapt, with reports of crops left unpicked and a lack of staff in care homes and hospitals.

Another issue has been the impact on the rights of EU citizens already residing in the UK. The EU Settlement Scheme was established to protect their residency rights, but there have been complications and uncertainties for some individuals, with reports of difficulties accessing services and confusion over status.

The implications for family reunification have also been a contentious issue. The UK's immigration rules are now more restrictive for EU citizens who want to bring family members to the UK, leading to criticism from human rights organizations about the system's fairness and compassion.

Furthermore, the UK has sought to strengthen its borders and increase internal security, but this has sometimes clashed with the humanitarian need to provide asylum to refugees. The government's plans to send some asylum seekers to Rwanda for processing have drawn international scrutiny and debate over the ethics and legality of such actions.

Brexit has provided the UK with the autonomy to reshape its immigration policy, but it has also posed challenges in terms of economic impacts, human rights, and the UK's international reputation. The balance between control and openness remains a delicate and evolving issue.

The long-term effects of these policy changes are still unfolding, and it remains to be seen how the UK will navigate the complexities of a post-Brexit world, where it seeks to maintain a global presence while asserting its sovereign right to control immigration.
