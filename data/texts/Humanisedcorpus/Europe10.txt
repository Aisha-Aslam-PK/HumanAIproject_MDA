Britain's choice to leave the European Union, known as Brexit, marked a real change in its relationships at home and abroad. Before Brexit, the UK benefited from the EU's collective power in global trade and politics. After Brexit, the country must work on re-establishing its identity as an independent player in the world. This task presents both challenges and opportunities.

Supporters of Brexit promote the idea of "Global Britain" as a vision for the UK's role in the world. This idea includes making new trade deals, building connections with non-EU countries, and asserting the UK's influence internationally, free from EU policies. The UK has acted quickly to negotiate new trade agreements with countries such as Japan and Australia and is pursuing membership in the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP). These steps are important for showing the UK's commitment to free trade and its ability to function as an independent trading nation.

However, this shift comes with its own complications. The EU was the UK's biggest trading partner, and the transition period has brought disruptions in trade, regulatory hurdles, and uncertainty for businesses. Building new relationships takes time, and the benefits of new trade deals may not immediately compare to the trade volume with the EU. There are also fears that in its rush to secure deals, the UK might accept terms that are not as favorable as those it had while part of the EU.

The UK's role in international diplomacy and security is also vital to its global positioning. The EU often acted as a united force in global politics, and Britain played a key role in shaping its policies. Now, as an independent country, the UK must redefine its foreign policy goals. The government's "Integrated Review of Security, Defence, Development and Foreign Policy" outlines a plan for the UK to become a leader in science and technology, while promoting free trade and democracy.

The UK's status as a permanent member of the United Nations Security Council and an important member of NATO has not changed. These roles still provide platforms for international influence. The UK can also strengthen ties with the Commonwealth of Nations, a group of 54 member states, most of which were once part of the British Empire.

In summary, post-Brexit Britain's global positioning is an ongoing story. The UK is working through the challenges of establishing itself as an independent global player while dealing with the changes brought by leaving the EU. Its success will rely on securing beneficial trade deals, maintaining its diplomatic influence, and achieving the vision of a "Global Britain" that actively engages with the world.
