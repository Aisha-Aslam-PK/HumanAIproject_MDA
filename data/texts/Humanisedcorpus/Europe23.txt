Brexit has created major challenges for UK higher education. This sector has long benefitted from the UK’s membership in the European Union through student mobility, academic partnerships, and research funding. The drastic change has left institutions working hard to keep their international reputation and find new ways to collaborate and secure funding.

Before Brexit, UK universities attracted many students from the EU, thanks to the Erasmus+ program, which supported exchanges and created a multicultural campus atmosphere. Now, the UK’s decision not to take part in Erasmus+ after Brexit has raised worries about the future of student mobility in and out of the UK. The UK government has introduced the Turing Scheme as a replacement. It aims to fund global exchanges, but it is still unclear whether it can achieve the same reach and appeal as Erasmus+.

The effects on research funding are also significant. UK institutions were among the biggest recipients of EU research programs like Horizon 2020. Losing easy access to such collaborative funding is impactful. Although the UK has negotiated participation in Horizon Europe, the long-term relationship remains uncertain due to ongoing political tensions.

The UK's research environment is also facing a potential brain drain. The end of free movement and tougher immigration rules may hinder the ability to attract and keep talent. EU academics who were once eager to work in the UK now confront additional bureaucratic challenges. This may push them to look for jobs in places that feel more accessible.

To address these issues, universities are changing their international strategies. They are creating new partnerships outside of Europe, expanding their student recruitment efforts, and working to strengthen their global presence. Additionally, the government's plan to increase spending on research and development to 2.4% of GDP by 2027 is a move to fill the gap left by EU funding, aiming to make the UK a more appealing research location.

At the same time, higher education institutions are stressing the importance of international talent and advocating for immigration policies that make it easier for students and researchers to move. The launch of the Graduate Immigration Route, which lets international students stay in the UK for work after they finish their studies, is a positive change in this regard.

UK higher education stands at a critical point. It must redefine its international appeal and collaboration methods. The sector has a reputation for resilience and adaptability. However, it needs supportive policies and innovative ideas to retain its prestigious position in global academia after Brexit.
