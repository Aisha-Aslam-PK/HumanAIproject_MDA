The decision by the United Kingdom to leave the European Union was a key moment in the history of British immigration policy. Brexit was largely a vote on freedom of movement, a key principle of the EU that allowed EU citizens to live and work in the UK with few restrictions. With this arrangement ending, the UK had to create a new immigration system that reflected its identity after Brexit, managing border control while addressing the need for foreign workers.

The points-based immigration system introduced in early 2021 aimed to attract individuals who could help the UK economy, regardless of their nationality. It focused on skills and talents rather than nationality, marking a shift from the EU's preference-based system. This new approach was seen as a way to reclaim sovereignty by those who wanted stricter immigration controls, believing it would lead to lower arrival numbers and more selective, merit-based criteria.

However, this new system has faced criticism. There are concerns about sectors that traditionally depended on EU labor, like agriculture, healthcare, and hospitality, which are now experiencing labor shortages. These industries have struggled to adjust, with reports of unpicked crops and staff shortages in care homes and hospitals.

Another issue is the impact on the rights of EU citizens already living in the UK. The EU Settlement Scheme was set up to protect their residency rights, but some individuals have faced complications and uncertainties, including difficulties accessing services and confusion about their status.

Family reunification has also become a controversial topic. The UK's immigration rules are now stricter for EU citizens wanting to bring family members to the UK, drawing criticism from human rights organizations regarding the fairness and compassion of the system.

Moreover, the UK has aimed to bolster its borders and enhance internal security, but this sometimes clashes with the need to provide asylum to refugees. The government's plans to send some asylum seekers to Rwanda for processing have sparked international scrutiny and debate over the ethics and legality of these actions.

Brexit has given the UK the freedom to reshape its immigration policy, but it has also created challenges in terms of economic effects, human rights, and the country's international reputation. Balancing control and openness continues to be a sensitive and evolving issue.

The long-term impacts of these policy changes are still emerging, and it remains to be seen how the UK will deal with the complexities of a post-Brexit world, where it aims to maintain a global presence while asserting its right to control immigration.
